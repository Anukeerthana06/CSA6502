Industrial Motor Maintenance Manual (Sample) — IndustroSense AI Knowledge Base

Section 1: Overview
This manual covers general maintenance guidance for standard three-phase
induction motors used in industrial settings (pumps, fans, conveyors,
compressors). All figures below are illustrative sample values created
for a college laboratory demonstration and must not be used for real
equipment decisions.

Section 2: Motor Overheating
Motor overheating is commonly associated with one or more of the
following causes: sustained electrical overload beyond nameplate rating,
poor ventilation or blocked cooling fins, insufficient or degraded
lubrication in the bearings, voltage imbalance across phases, or
prolonged operation in a high ambient-temperature environment. A motor
that becomes noticeably hot to the touch after approximately 20 to 30
minutes of continuous operation, especially when accompanied by a
burning odor or discoloration of the housing, should be shut down and
inspected before continued use. Continuing to run an overheating motor
can accelerate insulation breakdown and lead to winding failure.

Section 3: Vibration
Excessive motor vibration typically indicates one of: rotor imbalance,
shaft misalignment with the driven load, worn or damaged bearings, a
loose foundation or mounting bolts, or bent shaft. Vibration combined
with an audible grinding or rattling noise is frequently associated
with bearing wear and should prompt a bearing inspection. Vibration
alone, without abnormal noise or heat, may indicate a simpler
imbalance or alignment issue that can often be corrected without
disassembly of the motor.

Section 4: Lubrication
Motor bearings require periodic lubrication according to the
manufacturer's schedule. Under-lubrication accelerates bearing wear
and can contribute to both overheating and vibration. Over-lubrication
can also cause problems, including seal damage and grease
churning that raises bearing temperature. Always follow the specific
lubricant type and quantity recommended for the bearing size in use.

Section 5: Electrical Faults
Common electrical faults include phase imbalance, insulation
degradation, loose terminal connections, and winding short circuits.
Phase imbalance greater than a few percent can cause uneven heating
across the three windings and should be investigated with a clamp
meter. Loose terminal connections are a frequent, easily corrected
cause of localized heating at the terminal box.

Section 6: Preventive Maintenance Interval
Preventive maintenance for general-purpose industrial motors in this
sample knowledge base follows a baseline interval of every 500
operating hours, covering visual inspection, vibration check,
temperature check, and lubrication top-up as needed. Motors in harsher
environments (high dust, high humidity, high ambient temperature) may
require shorter intervals as specified by the equipment-specific SOP.

Section 7: Safety Notice
Before performing any maintenance, the motor must be de-energized and
locked out/tagged out (LOTO) according to facility procedure. Never
bypass thermal protection devices, emergency stop circuits, or
interlocks to keep a motor running. If a motor shows signs of a
serious electrical fault, insulation failure, or repeated overheating,
qualified electrical maintenance personnel must inspect it before
restart.
