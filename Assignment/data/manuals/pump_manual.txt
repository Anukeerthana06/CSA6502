Industrial Centrifugal Pump Manual (Sample) — IndustroSense AI Knowledge Base

Section 1: Overview
This sample manual describes general maintenance guidance for
centrifugal pumps used in industrial fluid-transfer applications. All
values are illustrative for a laboratory demonstration.

Section 2: Pump Vibration
Pump vibration is often caused by cavitation (insufficient suction
pressure causing vapor bubbles to collapse inside the pump), impeller
imbalance, misalignment between pump and driver, worn bearings, or
loose foundation bolts. Cavitation typically produces a distinctive
rattling or "gravel" sound near the pump inlet along with vibration,
and is often resolved by checking suction-side piping for restrictions
or air entrainment before assuming a mechanical fault.

Section 3: Seal and Bearing Lubrication
Mechanical seals rely on a thin fluid film and proper flush/cooling
arrangement; running a pump dry, even briefly, can damage the seal
faces. Bearing housings should be lubricated according to the
interval specified for the bearing type (grease or oil-lubricated),
and lubricant should never be mixed between incompatible types without
manufacturer guidance.

Section 4: Overheating and Reduced Flow
A pump that overheats or shows reduced output flow may be
experiencing impeller wear, a clogged strainer or suction line,
excessive discharge-side throttling, or entrained air in the suction
line. Running a centrifugal pump against a closed discharge valve for
an extended period causes rapid internal heat buildup and should be
avoided.

Section 5: Preventive Maintenance Interval
Baseline preventive maintenance for general industrial centrifugal
pumps in this sample knowledge base follows a 500-operating-hour
interval: check for leaks, verify vibration levels, inspect coupling
alignment, and confirm seal/bearing lubrication status.

Section 6: Safety Notice
Pumps handling hazardous, hot, or pressurized fluids must be
depressurized, drained, and isolated per facility lockout/tagout
procedure before any seal or bearing work. Do not disable pressure
relief devices or discharge safety valves to work around a
suspected blockage.
